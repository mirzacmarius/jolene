# The workshop reads the orders Convex keeps, for the team's accounts

Orders are Convex's since Jolene sells through its own checkout (docs/adr/0015): each paid Checkout Session becomes an order with its lines as they were priced, each designed line with the proof the couple approved (docs/adr/0007), who paid, where it goes and what Stripe charged. The workshop needs them to make and send what was ordered, and no one else may see them: an order holds a customer's name, email, phone and address.

So the studio has the workshop's pages, `/studio/atelier` for the list and `/studio/atelier/J1001` for one order, and Convex answers them only for the team:

- **Queries over the orders, not a copy.** The list reads the `orders` table newest first, a page at a time (`workshop.orders`), and an order's page reads one by its number (`workshop.order`). A paid order shows the moment it's recorded, and nothing keeps a second record of it. Until the checkout moved, the list read Shopify's orders through the Admin API each time it opened; none of that remains.
- **The team signs in as couples do.** An emailed code (docs/adr/0011) proves the address is theirs, and the pages open to the accounts whose address is in `WORKSHOP_EMAILS` on the Convex deployment. Every query checks it (`requireWorkshop` in `convex/workshop/staff.ts`). Google sign-in was the first plan: a code needs no OAuth client, works on every preview and on any address the studio is served from, and is one way in for the team to learn. The team can now sign in with Google as well (docs/adr/0030).
- **What an order's page shows**: each line with its proof's picture or the product's photo, its quantity, price and options, the answers the couple wrote, the event a digital invitation was bought for, and a print file for each designed line (docs/adr/0010); the fees, delivery, discount, total and the VAT in it; who paid, the language their emails speak, and where it goes; links to the couple's order page and to the payment in Stripe's dashboard.
- **Statuses the team moves on.** An order is paid, then being made, then sent by courier or ready to pick up, then complete; or cancelled. One with nothing to deliver, such as a digital invitation alone, is complete once it's paid. The order's page has a button for its next step and a menu that puts it at any other status it can have, so a step pressed too soon is undone the same way. `workshop.setStatus` takes the status the page showed and changes nothing if someone moved the order since (`order-moved`), so two people can't move it on twice. Each change, and each note the team writes on the order, goes in `orderHistory` with the account that made it, and the page lists them newest first. The couple's order page shows the same steps, never the notes.
- **An order going by courier is sent from its page.** It has no button to mark it sent: the team sends it by booking its courier (`workshop.bookCourier`), for the usual box or one it weighed and measured, or by typing the AWB of a courier it booked itself (`workshop.recordAwb`), as it must while Colete Online isn't set up (docs/adr/0019). Either moves a paid or being-made order on to sent in the same transaction, in the history under the account that did it, and the couple get the AWB by email. The page then shows the courier, the AWB, the label to print and the courier's latest word, and asks Colete Online again where the parcel is. If Colete Online refuses the booking, the page says why, and the team books again or types the AWB. A typed AWB can be corrected, and the couple get the new one. Marking a pickup order ready emails its couple that they can come for it.
- **The invoice.** The page shows the order's invoice and the credit note that reverses it after a full refund, each with its PDF (docs/adr/0018), and asks SmartBill again for an invoice it refused, never issued, or left unanswered for a quarter of an hour (`invoices.retry`).
- **Refunds through Stripe.** An order's page gives back part or all of what the couple paid, up to what's left (`workshop.refund`), and Stripe sends it to the card that paid. The idempotency key Stripe gets names the order, how many refunds it has had and the amount, so a double press makes one refund and a colleague's same refund at the same moment is refused (`same-refund`). Convex keeps each refund in `refunds` as Stripe last said it was, with the account that asked for it, and `orders.refunded` sums those that give money back: all but the failed or canceled ones. Stripe's webhook reads a payment's refunds again on `refund.created`, `refund.updated` and `refund.failed`, so a refund made in Stripe's dashboard and each refund's later news reach the order. The history lists each refund beside the changes and notes, and the couple's page says what went back to their card.

## Consequences

- Each deployment lists the team's addresses in `WORKSHOP_EMAILS`, production included; while it's unset, the pages open to no one. A preview writes its sign-in codes to its logs (`AUTH_LOG_CODES`), so whoever reads those can sign in there as a listed address; previews take test payments only.
- A team member's account is an account like any other: designs made in that browser before signing in move to it.
- Orders paid in Stripe's test mode show with a Test mark.
- The Shopify list checked each line against the catalog, since the shop's cart set the extras' price there. Convex prices every line itself when the couple goes to pay, from its own catalog, so those checks have nothing left to catch.
- An order's status is a field on it (`orders.status`), indexed with its number so each status lists newest first. Orders paid before statuses had none until the seed gave them their first (`convex/workshop/statuses.ts`); until it runs they read as paid, or complete when there's nothing to deliver.
- The status menu changes the status and nothing else, but for ready, which emails a pickup's couple: marking a courier's order sent from it books no courier and emails no one, for a parcel that left some other way.
- A refused booking leaves the order sent, since its parcel is packed: the team books again, types the AWB, or moves it back from the menu.
- A refund leaves the order's status alone, so the team cancels it when nothing more will be made, and a digital invitation it unlocked stays unlocked (docs/adr/0014). Only a full refund reverses the invoice, with a credit note (docs/adr/0018).
- Stripe's key needs Write on Refunds. Without it the page says so (`refund-not-allowed`), and the webhook logs a warning and still answers 200, as Stripe would otherwise send the event again for days on the endpoint that also records orders. A refund Stripe refuses, such as of a payment its key can't find, is refused with Stripe's reason in Convex's logs (`refund-refused`).
- A payment that isn't one of the deployment's orders, such as a preview's while production also takes test payments, has its refund events answered and left alone.
